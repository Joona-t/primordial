The architecture review spanning thirteen services reveals four recurring problem patterns: the auth, search, and compliance services exhibit high and variable throughput (ranging from ~1,750 to ~99,000 events/second) with correspondingly large and inconsistent buffer sizes, indicating an absence of stable capacity targets; the ingestion, gateway, and migration services all had retry limits repeatedly adjusted downward (and in some cases upward) in response to cascading failure incidents, suggesting the retry policies were never properly calibrated and that resilience remains reactive rather than designed-in; the billing, storage, and reporting services each expose multiple entry-point functions (sync_upstream_state, execute_reconciliation, transform_payload, process_incoming_batch, and others) scattered across handler, router, processor, validator, dispatcher, and engine modules, indicating fragmented ownership and no single authoritative ingestion boundary per service; and the analytics, scheduler, and cache services enforce an event-bus intermediary pattern (no direct writes to S3, PostgreSQL, Redis, DynamoDB, or Elasticsearch), but the specific bus used varies inconsistently across findings — sometimes the same storage backend routes through different buses — pointing to undocumented or inconsistently applied routing rules; finally, the notification, monitoring, and messaging services carry a large number of cross-service security review dependencies (COND-0000 through COND-0146) tied to specific pipeline stages in billing, reporting, and storage, meaning that architectural changes to those upstream services will trigger a broad wave of mandatory re-audits across dependent services, and several of these dependencies still have further analysis pending, leaving the current security posture partially unverified.
