The architecture review across 15 services reveals several systemic patterns: the auth service handles variable throughput ranging from roughly 66K to 97K events per second with inconsistent buffer sizing, while the search and compliance services operate at lower and more variable throughput tiers; multiple services (ingestion, gateway, migration) have had retry limits adjusted post-incident in response to cascading failures, though the gateway retry changes are anomalous in that some limits were raised rather than lowered, suggesting inconsistent incident response; analytics, cache, and scheduler services are uniformly decoupled from direct database writes, routing all persistence through intermediate event buses (DynamoDB, Elasticsearch, S3, or Redis) which adds resilience but introduces latency and operational complexity; billing, storage, and reporting services each expose multiple entry-point functions spread across core engine, handler, and dispatcher modules without a clear single entry point, indicating either multi-tenant routing logic or accumulated technical debt; and the notification and messaging services carry explicit security audit dependencies on upstream architectural stages, meaning any changes to reporting, billing, or storage architecture will trigger mandatory re-audits across specific pipeline conditions, creating a tightly coupled compliance burden that must be tracked carefully during future refactors.
